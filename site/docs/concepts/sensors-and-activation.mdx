A drag starts from the draggable's **activator**: its handle when it has one, the element itself
otherwise. Two sensors listen there, never on a global: the pointer sensor and the
[keyboard sensor](/docs/concepts/keyboard-and-accessibility).

## One path for mouse, touch and pen

The pointer sensor uses Pointer Events with `setPointerCapture`. There is no HTML5 drag and drop
and no drag library. A press is a click until it passes the activation, then a drag that follows
the pointer until it is released, cancelled or loses its capture.

## Activation

`activation` is per draggable:

| option | input | default | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `distance` | mouse, pen | `3` | px the pointer moves before the drag starts; `0` starts on press |
| `delay` | touch | `200` | ms a finger holds still before the drag starts |
| `tolerance` | touch | `5` | px a finger may move during the delay; more, and it scrolls the page instead |

```tsx title="touch.tsx"
<Draggable id={item} activation={{ delay: 250, tolerance: 8 }}>
    <li>{item}</li>
</Draggable>
```

A press under the activation is a click: `onClick` works as usual. The click that a drag's release
would produce is swallowed, so releasing a drag over a button does not press it.

## What cancels a drag

Escape, `pointercancel`, a lost pointer capture, a release where no `pointerup` arrives, and
`space.run("cancel")`. A cancelled drag reports `onDragCancel` and never `onDrop`; the element goes
back where it was (its `translate` is cleared).

## What never starts a drag

Native controls inside the activator: `input`, `textarea`, `select`, `button` (unless the button
is the handle itself), and editable content (`[contenteditable]`). Any subtree marked
`data-dnd-no-drag` opts out too, for text the user selects or a control of another library.

```tsx title="card.tsx"
<Draggable id="release">
    <article>
        <input aria-label="Title" />
        <button type="button">Upvote</button>
        <p data-dnd-no-drag="">Selectable notes</p>
    </article>
</Draggable>
```

A [`DragHandle`](/docs/api/drag-handle) makes the handle the only place a drag starts.

## The press claim, and the focus it gives back

A press that may become a drag is **claimed** on `pointerdown` with `preventDefault()`, not at
activation. That is how nested gestures compose: an outer library deciding on the same
`pointerdown` whether to start its own gesture sees `defaultPrevented` and stays still (Grid
Layout does, so a card inside a grid item drags the card, not the item). In turn, dnd ignores a
press another library already claimed.

Cancelling `pointerdown` has side effects, measured in Chromium and Firefox:

- **The compatibility mouse events are suppressed**: `mousedown` and `mouseup` do not fire for a
  claimed press. An `onMouseDown` on a draggable (or inside its activator, outside a control) will
  not run; use `onPointerDown`, which does. `click` still fires.
- **The focus a press gives is suppressed.** The sensor gives it back itself: to the press's
  closest focusable element inside the activator, as the browser would have.
- **Text selection** that a mouse press would start is prevented while the press lasts, on
  purpose.

Native controls and `data-dnd-no-drag` subtrees are never claimed, so they keep the browser's own
press, focus and selection.

## Touch scrolling

A touch that moves before its delay scrolls the page, so a list of draggables stays scrollable.
Once a touch drag runs, the page does not scroll under it. A handle carries
`touch-action: none`, so a touch on a handle never scrolls.
